# Adaptive Budget Allocation for Smart-Contract Fuzzing: Why Online Feedback Fails to Improve Vulnerability Triage

_arXiv ID: 2611.22430_  
_Authors: Yanzi Liu, Jiachi Tong_  
_Published: 2026-11-17_  
_Note: Accepted to the 22nd International Conference on Mobility, Sensing and Networking (MSN 2026). Camera-ready version. 8 pages, 5 figures_  
_Source: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22430v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22430v1)_

---

## Abstract

Smart-contract fuzzing campaigns share a finite budget and vary widely in the execution cost of a first correct-class vulnerability report. Using trace replay on 143 annotated and 350 bug-injected contracts, we test whether online feedback improves triage. At a mean budget of 100 executions per contract, uniform allocation reaches 72% and 70% of the respective clairvoyant optima. All eight online policies trail uniform on aggregate triage, as do the seven repeated on the bug-injected corpus. Five online signals yield ranking AUCs of 0.32-0.51 for imminent reports. Synthetic-oracle experiments show that sufficiently accurate detection-cost estimates recover more of the gap than detectability rankings. This motivates an offline probability-curve allocator that predicts first-report probabilities across budgets and allocates by predicted gain per execution. In a frozen evaluation on four fresh seeds per corpus, detection-budget AUC improves over uniform by 5.38% and 4.81% on the two source corpora and by 1.99-6.54% on three additional public benchmark subsets.

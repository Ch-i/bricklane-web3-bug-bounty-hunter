# Keeping Fuzzers on Duty: Repairing Fuzzing Build Breakages in Continuous Open-Source Fuzzing

_arXiv ID: 2611.06358_  
_Authors: Zhuang Zhao, Jiongchi Yu, Yao Zhang, Dongge Liu, Qiang Hu, Xiaohong Li, Yang Liu_  
_Published: 2026-11-04_  
_Note: 13 pages, 8 figures_  
_Source: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06358v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06358v1)_

## Abstract

Continuous fuzzing platforms such as OSS-Fuzz provide sustained security testing for open-source projects, but long-term build breakages reduce security coverage and waste infrastructure resources. In OSS-Fuzz, around 20% of integrated projects remain in build-failure states, leaving their fuzzers off duty. These failures expose a maintenance gap between platform maintainers, who lack project-specific context, and project maintainers, who often lack fuzzing and infrastructure expertise. Existing program-repair techniques cannot be directly applied because fuzz build breakages span evolving project and infrastructure artifacts and require repeated infrastructure-aware diagnosis. We present FuzzMedic, an automated agent for fuzz build repair. FuzzMedic performs evidence-guided diagnosis across project and fuzzing-infrastructure artifacts. It explores repairs over a repair-state graph (RSG) that records the evolving workspace and build feedback. Branching and rollback enable FuzzMedic to recover from unproductive repair paths during long-horizon debugging. Candidate patches are validated to restore executable fuzz targets rather than merely suppress build failures. We evaluate FuzzMedic on 128 reproducible OSS-Fuzz build failures extracted from 1,820 real-world failures. FuzzMedic achieves a 48.44% repair rate and outperforms three representative program-repair baselines. In real-world use, it produces 29 patches merged into upstream or OSS-Fuzz main branches, including repairs for three failures that had remained unresolved for more than one year and two cases that had never built successfully.

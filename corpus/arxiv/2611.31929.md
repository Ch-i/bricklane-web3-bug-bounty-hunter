## Abstract

In LLM-based smart contract auditing, detection quality depends on both model capability and workflow design. While coding agents can revise workflows from execution feedback, unconstrained rewrites rarely yield stable gains. Cross-function vulnerabilities require multi-step state tracking and boundary reasoning that coarse pipelines miss. End-to-end outcomes hide which step lost a clue, so direct rewrites regress existing hits or discard valid edits under coarse aggregate scoring. Repeatedly iterating on a small project set also adds case-specific heuristics that bloat prompts and hurt generalization. We present Shuttle, a constrained workflow-evolution system, and LucidScan, the auditing framework it evolves. Shuttle centers on provenance-graph attribution: it expands each run into an instance-level dependency graph, locates the node where a clue was lost via forward search, and traces existing hits backward into a protected set. After evaluating a merged batch, Shuttle drops dragging edits via a node-level provenance ledger, falling back to cache-reusing local re-runs only when early-round edits overlap on the same node. For localized bottlenecks, Shuttle generates edits under task decomposition, progressive refinement, and generality blind review. On 10 training projects, Shuttle evolves LucidScan from $G_0$ (28.7% per-project average C/H/M recall) to $G_8$ (82.1%). On 13 held-out validation projects under the same model, average recall rises from 28.6% ($G_0$) to 70.3% ($G_8$), above Pashov and Codex Security. In an anonymized live public audit contest with 896 researchers (Nov. 2026), the evolved scanner tied for #1 in High-severity recall (6/8, 75.0%), ranked #2 in scored H/M clusters (34/72, 47.2%) with 92.9% validity (78/84), and finished #3 overall.

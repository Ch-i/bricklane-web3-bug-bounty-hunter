# From Formal Methods to Fuzzing and Back

_arXiv ID: 2611.12338_  
_Authors: Alastair F. Donaldson_  
_Published: 2026-11-09_  
_Source: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12338v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12338v1)_

---

## Abstract

For the first twelve years of my career in computer science I mainly worked on formal methods. I had been drawn to the field as an undergraduate by its promise of a way to build software with strong, mathematically based correctness guarantees. But after many years of struggling to make formal methods scale, I was seduced by randomised testing---also known as fuzzing. Fuzzing is much less principled than formal methods, but it just works: it can be readily applied to very large systems, and it is extremely effective at finding real bugs that developers care about and fix. In this personal account I trace my path from formal methods to fuzzing, by way of the GPUVerify project on verifying GPU kernels and the GraphicsFuzz project on randomised testing of GPU compilers. I argue that the oracle problem provides an intellectual link between fuzzing and formal methods, describe my continuing, targeted uses of formal methods in the domain of GPU programming language design, and discuss work at the boundary between formal methods and fuzzing. Finally, I reflect on whether generative AI might be the missing ingredient that allows formal methods to scale, and set out my plans for large-scale formal methods via the Keystone project, through which my collaborators and I aim to find out how much of that promise can be realised in practice.
